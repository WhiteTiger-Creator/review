THE LINK RULES
VLAN INTERFACES, ADDRESS RECORDS AND THE CONTEST (N113 through N142)

--- VLAN interfaces (N113 through N120) ---

N113  A VLAN interface STANDS where it has been raised and has not been lowered since. At the
open it stands where the VLAN table wrote it yes, by N017 read for VLANs at N023.

N114  A RAISE STEP naming a VLAN makes it stand from that instant, and from that instant onward
it HAS BEEN RAISED AT SOME MOMENT; that reading is never undone. A LOWER STEP naming a VLAN
makes it stop standing from that instant. Neither step touches its parent, its tag, or any
record declared on it. A raise on a VLAN that already stands and a lower on a VLAN that already
does not stand change nothing anywhere.

N115  A VLAN IS RULED WITHOUT ANY REFERENCE TO ITS PARENT. THIS IS ONE OF THE PLACES THESE
RULES PART COMPANY WITH ORDINARY PRACTICE, where a VLAN interface follows its lower device and
goes down with it. HERE THE PARENT'S STATE IS NEVER WEIGHED AT ALL. A VLAN is settled by its
own raise and lower steps and by the records standing on it, and by nothing else. The parent is
read in exactly one place in this rulebook, the stack depth at N126, and it is printed in the
VLAN block.

N116  EVERY VLAN THE VLAN TABLE NAMES takes EXACTLY ONE state out of this closed set of four.
The rungs are weighed IN THIS ORDER and the FIRST one that answers is the state:
      rung 1  it has NEVER been raised at any moment                          UNMADE
      rung 2  it does not stand at the close                                  DOWN
      rung 3  it stands and AT LEAST ONE record declared on it is STANDING    UP
      rung 4  it stands and no record declared on it is STANDING              QUIET

N117  THE STANDING-RECORD COLUMN of a VLAN line is the number of records DECLARED ON THAT VLAN
whose state at the close is STANDING, by N138. It counts standing records and never claimed
records, so a claimed record that was ousted or set aside is not counted here.

N118  A VLAN LINE CARRIES NO RULE TOKEN, because each of the four VLAN states has exactly one
cause and there is nothing for a token to distinguish. THE VLAN BLOCK HAS NO TOKEN COLUMN and
no reader should look for one.

N119  A VLAN WHOSE PARENT IS UNMADE, DOWN, STINTED, OR A PHYSICAL INTERFACE THAT NEVER STOOD is
exactly as its own raise and lower steps left it, by N115, and every record declared on it is
weighed in its contest EXACTLY AS IF THE PARENT STOOD. No record is dropped, weakened or moved
because of what happened to a parent.

N120  A VLAN THAT WAS LOWERED AND STILL HOLDS A STANDING RECORD is DOWN by rung 2 of N116, and
that record is STANDING by N138. Its standing-record column still shows the record. The VLAN
and the record are ruled apart and neither drags the other.

--- Claim and release (N121 through N125) ---

N121  A CLAIM STEP names an address text and an interface, which together name exactly one
record of the address table, by N028. That record is CLAIMED from that instant.

N122  A CLAIM STEP NAMING A RECORD THAT IS ALREADY CLAIMED CHANGES NOTHING ANYWHERE.

N123  A RELEASE STEP names an address text and an interface and makes that record stop being
claimed from that instant. A RELEASE STEP NAMING A RECORD THAT IS NOT CLAIMED CHANGES NOTHING
ANYWHERE.

N124  A RECORD RELEASED AND THEN CLAIMED AGAIN is claimed at the close, so it takes its full
part in its contest and IT IS NOT DROPPED. The release leaves no mark of any kind.

N125  A record HAS BEEN CLAIMED AT SOME MOMENT where the address table wrote it yes at the
open, or where a claim step reached it at any point of the run. This reading is never undone by
a later release step.

--- Stack depth (N126 through N128) ---

N126  THE STACK DEPTH of a record is the depth of the interface the record is declared on, and
that depth is:
      a PHYSICAL INTERFACE                 depth 1
      a BOND                               depth 2
      a VLAN                               ONE MORE than the depth of its parent
The depth of an interface never changes during the run and does not read any state: a bond
counts two whether it is FULL, STINTED, DOWN or UNMADE, and a physical interface counts one
whether its link stands or not.

N127  Because a parent is never another VLAN, by N024, a VLAN over a physical interface counts
depth 2 and a VLAN over a bond counts depth 3, and no depth is ever weighed further than that.
A VLAN OVER A PHYSICAL INTERFACE AND A BOND THEREFORE COUNT THE SAME DEPTH, so a tie on rung
two of N135 is an ordinary occurrence and not a rare one.

N128  The depth column of the address block prints this number.

--- Set-aside on a bond in reduced standing (N129 through N132) ---

N129  SET-ASIDE. Where a bond is in reduced standing at the close, take the records declared on
that bond that are CLAIMED AT THE CLOSE. The one standing HIGHEST IN THE ADDRESS TABLE, that
is, the FIRST DECLARED, with the LOWEST RANK, keeps its place and is weighed in its contest
under N135. EVERY OTHER ONE of them is SET-ASIDE. Only records on a bond in reduced standing
are ever set aside; a record on a physical interface or on a VLAN never is, whatever the parent
is doing.

N130  A SET-ASIDE RECORD IS OUT OF ITS CONTEST ALTOGETHER. It is not weighed on any rung, it
cannot take its address, and it would not have taken it even if no other record named that
address. It is not counted by the standing-record column of any VLAN and it does not make any
interface BEARING.

N131  A BOND IN REDUCED STANDING WHOSE ONLY CLAIMED RECORD IS ITS TOP-RANKED ONE SETS NOTHING
ASIDE. That record stays in its contest and is weighed LAST within it by rung one of N135.

N132  A BOND IN REDUCED STANDING WITH NO CLAIMED RECORD AT ALL sets nothing aside and nothing
is weighed for it.

--- The contest (N133 through N137) ---

N133  THE CONTESTING RECORDS for an address text are the records naming that address that are
CLAIMED AT THE CLOSE and NOT SET ASIDE. THIS IS ONE OF THE PLACES THESE RULES PART COMPANY WITH
ORDINARY PRACTICE, where an address on an interface that is not up is treated as gone. HERE A
RECORD IS RULED WITHOUT ANY REFERENCE TO WHETHER THE INTERFACE IT NAMES STANDS. A record on a
DARK interface, on an UNWIRED interface, on a DOWN bond, on a STINTED bond, on an UNMADE bond
or on a DOWN VLAN is in its contest exactly like any other, and it can be the record that takes
the address.

N134  WHERE EXACTLY ONE RECORD CONTESTS AN ADDRESS it takes that address and is STANDING with
the token stand.sole, and no rung of N135 is weighed. This holds whether the other records
naming that address were never claimed, released, or set aside.

N135  THE CONTEST. Where two or more records contest one address they are weighed on these
three rungs, IN THIS ORDER AND NO OTHER, and the first rung that separates them decides:
      RUNG ONE, THE REDUCED-STANDING RUNG. A record declared on a bond that is in reduced
      standing at the close is weighed AFTER every contesting record that is not. So a record
      that is not on such a bond beats one that is, always.
      RUNG TWO, THE DEPTH RUNG. THE DEEPER RECORD WINS: the record whose stack depth by N126 is
      the LARGER number takes the address ahead of one whose depth is smaller.
      RUNG THREE, THE TABLE RUNG. Where records tie on both rungs above, THE RECORD STANDING
      LOWEST IN THE ADDRESS TABLE, that is, THE LAST ONE DECLARED, with the HIGHEST RANK, takes
      the address.
THIS IS ONE OF THE PLACES THESE RULES PART COMPANY WITH ORDINARY PRACTICE, where a duplicate
address is settled by which interface claimed it first or by which claimed it last. THE MOMENT
A RECORD WAS CLAIMED IS NEVER WEIGHED HERE, on any rung.

N136  Exactly one contesting record takes the address. It is STANDING with the token stand.won.
EVERY OTHER CONTESTING RECORD IS OUSTED, and its token names the rung on which it lost, by
N140. Where three or more records contest one address, exactly one is STANDING and every other
one is OUSTED.

N137  WHERE EVERY CONTESTING RECORD FOR ONE ADDRESS SITS ON BONDS IN REDUCED STANDING, rung one
separates nobody, so the contest falls through to rung two and then to rung three, and one of
them does take the address. Rung one never leaves an address unheld.

--- The address-record state ladder (N138 through N142) ---

N138  EVERY RECORD THE ADDRESS TABLE DECLARES takes EXACTLY ONE state out of this closed set of
five. The rungs are weighed IN THIS ORDER and the FIRST one that answers is the state:
      rung 1  it has NEVER been claimed at any moment                      UNCLAIMED
      rung 2  it was claimed at some moment and is not claimed at the close  DROPPED
      rung 3  it is set aside by N129                                       SET-ASIDE
      rung 4  it contested its address and another record took it           OUSTED
      rung 5  it is claimed at the close and it holds its address           STANDING

N139  Every record prints exactly one RULE TOKEN out of this closed set of eight:
      STANDING   stand.sole  no other record contested its address, by N134
                 stand.won   it took a contest of two or more, by N136
      OUSTED     oust.stinted  it lost on rung one of N135
                 oust.depth    it lost on rung two
                 oust.place    it lost on rung three
      SET-ASIDE  aside.stinted
      DROPPED    drop.released
      UNCLAIMED  cold.never-claimed

N140  THE OUSTED TOKEN NAMES THE RUNG IT LOST ON, and the three are weighed IN THIS ORDER, the
first that answers being the token: FIRST, where this record is on a bond in reduced standing
and the record that took the address is not, oust.stinted. SECOND, where the record that took
the address has a STRICTLY GREATER stack depth than this one, oust.depth. THIRD, in every other
case, oust.place.

N141  AN ADDRESS NAMED BY EXACTLY ONE RECORD IN THE WHOLE ADDRESS TABLE takes the N134 route:
that record is STANDING with stand.sole where it is claimed at the close and not set aside, and
otherwise it takes its state from N138 like any other record.

N142  Two readings that follow from this section and are stated here so that no reader has to
infer them. FIRST, AN INTERFACE THAT IS A BOND MEMBER AND IS ALSO NAMED BY ITS OWN ADDRESS
RECORDS takes its state from the bond rungs of N148, BEARING by carry or SPARE, and never
bear.own; its records are ruled here on their own and count depth 1. SECOND, AN INTERFACE THAT
NEVER STOOD AND IS NAMED BY A CLAIMED RECORD is UNWIRED, while the record takes its full part
in its contest and can be the record STANDING.
