THE LINK RULES
THE SELF CHECK, THE REPORT FORM AND THE CASES GATHERED (N143 through N160)

--- The self check (N143) ---

N143  Given the flag --selfcheck the tool settles the host TWICE over the same unchanged input
and files the report again. It proves ONLY that a repeat run agrees with itself, and it PROVES
NOTHING ABOUT WHETHER ANY STATE IS RIGHT: a settlement that is wrong in the same way twice
passes it. It prints NOTHING on standard output and NOTHING on standard error and leaves the
status zero when the two agree.

--- The head line and the form of every line (N144 through N145) ---

N144  THE HEAD LINE is the first line of the report and is written as these words and values
separated by single spaces:
      host, the host name from host.conf, interfaces, the number of lines in
      links/interfaces.table, bonds, the number of lines in links/bonds.table, vlans, the number
      of lines in links/vlans.table, addresses, the number of lines in links/addresses.table,
      then the word carrying and the carrying count of N111.
So the head line reads: host NAME interfaces N bonds N vlans N addresses N carrying N.
None of these counts reads the recorded run; every one of them is fixed by the declared estate.

N145  EVERY LINE OF EVERY BLOCK is written as that block's fields in the order its clause gives,
with ONE SPACE between fields, each field padded to the width its clause gives. A field written
LEFT holds its value at the left of the width with spaces after it. A field written RIGHT holds
its value at the right of the width with spaces before it. A value longer than its width is
written whole and is not cut. NO LINE CARRIES BLANK SPACE AT ITS END, so the padding of the
last field of a line is dropped. THE HEADER LINE OF EVERY BLOCK is that block's column names
written into those very same columns by this same rule, so a header line is derived and never
invented.

--- The interface block (N146 through N149) ---

N146  The interface block header is the interface block's column names in the interface block's
columns. The names, in order, are iface, slot, bond, plc, state, rule. Written out by N145 the
header line is exactly, between the marks and not including them:
      |iface        slot   bond         plc state    rule|

N147  The interface block holds ONE LINE PER PHYSICAL INTERFACE the interface table names, in
INTERFACE TABLE ORDER, TOP LINE FIRST, and its six fields are:
      iface           LEFT 12   the interface name
      slot            LEFT  6   its slot from the interface table
      bond            LEFT 12   the bond whose ladder holds it at the close, a SINGLE HYPHEN
                                where no ladder holds it
      plc            RIGHT  3   its place on that ladder by N060, a SINGLE HYPHEN where no
                                ladder holds it
      state           LEFT  8   its state from the ladder at N148
      rule            LEFT 18   its rule token from N149
The bond and plc columns read ENROLMENT and not standing, so a member of a bond that does not
stand still prints that bond and that place, by N085.

N148  EVERY PHYSICAL INTERFACE takes EXACTLY ONE state out of this closed set of five. The
rungs are weighed IN THIS ORDER and the FIRST one that answers is the state:
      rung 1  its link has NEVER stood at any moment, by N039                UNWIRED
      rung 2  its link does not stand at the close                           DARK
      rung 3  a bond's ladder holds it and it holds that bond's carry        BEARING
      rung 4  a bond's ladder holds it and it does not hold the carry        SPARE
      rung 5  no ladder holds it and at least one record declared on it is
              STANDING at the close                                          BEARING
      rung 6  no ladder holds it and no record declared on it is STANDING    BARE
Rungs 3 and 4 read ENROLMENT, so they answer for a member of a bond that does not stand and for
a member of a bond in reduced standing. An interface a ladder holds NEVER reaches rung 5, so
holding a standing address never lifts a bond member out of SPARE, and an interface whose link
does not stand never reaches rung 3, 4, 5 or 6 at all, however many standing records it holds.

N149  Every physical interface prints exactly one RULE TOKEN out of this closed set of eight.
Under each state the rungs are weighed IN THIS ORDER and the FIRST that answers is the token:
      BEARING  rung 1  it holds a bond's carry                              bear.carry
               rung 2  no ladder holds it and a record on it is STANDING    bear.own
      SPARE    rung 1  its bond's carry stands vacant, by N066              spare.vacant
               rung 2  another member of its bond holds the carry           spare.ladder
      BARE     rung 1  it left a bond by a detach step at some moment of
                       the run, by N056                                     bare.detached
               rung 2  it never left a bond by a detach step                bare.idle
      DARK     rung 1  always                                              dark.link-down
      UNWIRED  rung 1  always                                              cold.never-up

--- The bond block (N150 through N151) ---

N150  The bond block header is the bond block's column names in the bond block's columns. The
names, in order, are bond, dec, enr, cnt, flr, bearer, state, rule. Written out by N145 the
header line is exactly, between the marks and not including them:
      |bond         dec enr cnt flr bearer       state    rule|

N151  The bond block holds ONE LINE PER BOND the bond table names, in BOND TABLE ORDER, TOP LINE
FIRST, and its eight fields are:
      bond            LEFT 12   the bond name
      dec            RIGHT  3   its declared column by N064
      enr            RIGHT  3   its enrolled column by N061
      cnt            RIGHT  3   its counted column by N062
      flr            RIGHT  3   its member floor from the bond table
      bearer          LEFT 12   the member bearing for it at the close, a SINGLE HYPHEN where
                                the carry stands vacant, by N074
      state           LEFT  8   its state from the ladder at N097
      rule            LEFT 18   its rule token from N103

--- The VLAN block (N152 through N153) ---

N152  The VLAN block header is the VLAN block's column names in the VLAN block's columns. The
names, in order, are vlan, parent, tag, std, state. Written out by N145 the header line is
exactly, between the marks and not including them:
      |vlan         parent         tag std state|

N153  The VLAN block holds ONE LINE PER VLAN the VLAN table names, in VLAN TABLE ORDER, TOP LINE
FIRST, and its five fields are:
      vlan            LEFT 12   the VLAN name
      parent          LEFT 12   its parent from the VLAN table
      tag            RIGHT  5   its tag from the VLAN table
      std            RIGHT  3   its standing-record column by N117
      state           LEFT  6   its state from the ladder at N116
THERE IS NO RULE TOKEN COLUMN IN THIS BLOCK, by N118. The line ends at the state.

--- The address block (N154 through N156) ---

N154  The address block header is the address block's column names in the address block's
columns. The names, in order, are address, iface, dep, state, rule. Written out by N145 the
header line is exactly, between the marks and not including them:
      |address            iface        dep state      rule|

N155  The address block holds ONE LINE PER RECORD the address table declares, and its five
fields are:
      address         LEFT 18   the address text
      iface           LEFT 12   the interface the record is declared on
      dep            RIGHT  2   its stack depth by N126
      state           LEFT 10   its state from the ladder at N138
      rule            LEFT 18   its rule token from N139
THE ADDRESS BLOCK IS SORTED ASCENDING BY ADDRESS TEXT in the byte order of N004, and where two
records carry the same address text, ASCENDING BY THE INTERFACE NAME in that same byte order.
IT IS DELIBERATELY NOT IN ADDRESS TABLE ORDER. The rank settles contests, by N135, and is never
printed and never sorted on. Byte order is not numeric order, so an address text beginning 10.20
sorts before one beginning 10.5.

N156  THE FOUR TALLY LINES close the report, in this order, each written as its heading, then
each state of its closed set with the number of lines in that state, all separated by single
spaces, IN THE ORDER THE CLOSED SET IS GIVEN HERE:
      interfaces BEARING N SPARE N BARE N DARK N UNWIRED N
      bonds FULL N THIN N STINTED N DOWN N UNMADE N
      vlans UP N QUIET N DOWN N UNMADE N
      addresses STANDING N OUSTED N SET-ASIDE N DROPPED N UNCLAIMED N
EVERY STATE IS PRINTED IN ITS TALLY EVEN WHERE ITS COUNT IS ZERO. No row is ever left out.

--- The shape of the file and the running discipline (N157 through N159) ---

N157  The report carries NO BLANK LINE ANYWHERE and ends with EXACTLY ONE TRAILING LINE FEED.
THE REPORT CARRIES NO SEAL OF ANY KIND: no digest, no hash, no checksum, no chained line and no
total computed over its own text.

N158  THE REPORT'S LINE COUNT is one head line, four block headers, one line per physical
interface the interface table names, one line per bond the bond table names, one line per VLAN
the VLAN table names, one line per record the address table declares, and four tally lines, AND
NOTHING ELSE. Because no step of the run can add or take away a declared object, by N033, THAT
COUNT IS FIXED ENTIRELY BY THE DECLARED ESTATE AND DOES NOT MOVE WHEN THE RECORDED RUN GETS
LONGER OR SHORTER. No count of steps, no list of steps and no term naming the run appears
anywhere in the report.

N159  THE RUNNING DISCIPLINE. The host's tables and its recorded run are read from beneath the
link root, which is the full directory path the environment names in LINK_ROOT, and where that
is unset it is /app/host. The report is written to the full FILE path the environment names in
LINK_STATE_REPORT, and where that is unset it is
/app/var/lib/linkctl/link-state-report.txt. Nothing is printed on standard output or standard
error on a run that files a report. Nothing beneath the link root is written, moved or removed.
THE DIRECTORY THE REPORT IS WRITTEN INTO IS CLEARED OF ALL PRE-EXISTING ENTRIES INCLUDING
SUBDIRECTORIES before the report is filed. Two runs over unchanged input file byte-identical
reports.

--- Every degenerate case gathered again (N160) ---

N160  These cases are settled where they stand and are gathered here so that a reader who has
read only the report form still meets every one of them.
      A RUN WITH NO STEPS AT ALL is settled exactly as the open left it, N034.
      A BOND WHOSE FLOOR IS ZERO is never STINTED, N089.
      A BOND WHOSE FLOOR EQUALS ITS DECLARED COUNT goes straight to STINTED, N107.
      A BOND OF ONE DECLARED MEMBER is not DOWN while that member is enrolled, N106.
      A BOND EVERY MEMBER OF WHICH HAS LOST ITS LINK is STINTED, not DOWN, N096.
      A BOND STANDING WITH AN EMPTY LADDER is DOWN even under a floor of zero, N108.
      A BOND LOWERED AND RAISED AGAIN is not DOWN, and its ladder was rebuilt, N086.
      AN ATTACH NAMING A MEMBER ALREADY ON THAT LADDER moves it to the top, N052.
      AN ATTACH NAMING A MEMBER ANOTHER BOND HOLDS is settled by the enrolment rule and may
      take nothing at all, N053.
      AN ATTACH ON A BOND THAT DOES NOT STAND does nothing, N050.
      A DETACH NAMING A MEMBER THAT BOND DOES NOT HOLD changes nothing, N055.
      A LINK-DOWN ON A LINK ALREADY DOWN, OR A LINK-UP ON ONE ALREADY UP, changes nothing, and
      a link-up on a link that never stood makes it stand, so it is not UNWIRED, N036 to N038.
      A VLAN OVER A DEAD OR STINTED OR UNMADE PARENT is ruled entirely on its own, N119.
      A VLAN THAT WAS LOWERED AND STILL HOLDS A STANDING RECORD is DOWN with that record shown
      in its standing-record column, N120.
      A CLAIM ON A RECORD ALREADY CLAIMED, OR A RELEASE ON ONE NOT CLAIMED, changes nothing,
      N122 and N123.
      A RECORD RELEASED AND CLAIMED AGAIN is not DROPPED, N124.
      AN ADDRESS NAMED BY EXACTLY ONE CONTESTING RECORD gives stand.sole with no contest, N134.
      AN ADDRESS NAMED BY THREE OR MORE RECORDS leaves exactly one STANDING and every other
      OUSTED with the token of the rung it lost on, N136 and N140.
      TWO CONTESTING RECORDS AT THE SAME DEPTH are separated by the LAST declared, N135, and
      that tie is ordinary because a VLAN over a physical interface and a bond both count two,
      N127.
      EVERY CONTESTING RECORD ON BONDS IN REDUCED STANDING falls through to depth and then to
      the table, N137.
      A BOND IN REDUCED STANDING WITH ONLY ITS TOP-RANKED RECORD CLAIMED sets nothing aside,
      N131, and one with no claimed record at all sets nothing aside, N132.
      AN INTERFACE THAT IS A BOND MEMBER AND ALSO CARRIES ITS OWN RECORDS is ruled on the bond
      rungs and never bear.own, N142 and N148.
      AN INTERFACE THAT NEVER STOOD AND CARRIES A CLAIMED RECORD is UNWIRED while the record
      takes its full part in the contest, N142.
