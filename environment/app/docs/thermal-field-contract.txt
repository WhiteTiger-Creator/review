Thermal field reconstruction contract
Revision 10

The QIR2 decoder produces detector counts in sensor row-major order. Frame metadata from SQLite, the QIR2 header, and GET /api/frames/{frameId} must agree before a frame is used. The sparse frame_checkpoints rows are independent PLC observations; every pixel_index must be in range and raw_count must equal the decoded detector count before repair. Checkpoint or reference rows for an unknown frame are invalid.

Camera calibration

GET /api/cameras/{cameraId}/calibration returns the radiometric coefficients. Only model quadratic-ambient-v2 is accepted. For detector count raw:

sensorC = offsetC + gain * raw + quadratic * raw * raw
temperatureC = ambientC + (sensorC - ambientC) / emissivity
               + ambientCoupling * (sensorC - ambientC)

Use floating-point raw values because a repaired detector may have a half-count value. Emissivity must be greater than zero. All coefficients and derived values must be finite.

GET /api/cameras/{cameraId}/geometry returns cameraId, revision, rotation, mirrorX, badPixels, hotSigma, minHotArea, homography, arrheniusA, activationEnergyJMol, detectorNoiseC, confidenceK, correlationMajorMm, correlationMinorMm, correlationAngleDeg, and temporalCorrelationSeconds. rotation must be 0, 90, 180, or 270 degrees clockwise. badPixels contains distinct in-range sensor indices. hotSigma must be finite and greater than zero. minHotArea must be positive. homography must contain exactly nine finite values. arrheniusA, activationEnergyJMol, detectorNoiseC, confidenceK, correlationMajorMm, and correlationMinorMm must be finite and greater than zero. correlationAngleDeg must be finite and is interpreted in degrees. temporalCorrelationSeconds must be finite and greater than zero.

Detector repair

Repair listed bad pixels in detector-count space before calibration. Each replacement uses the original decoded field; repaired values must not feed later repairs. Collect valid 8-neighbours, excluding positions outside the sensor and every listed bad pixel. At least three valid neighbours are required. Use the median of the neighbour counts; for an even count, average the two middle values. repairedPixels is the number of listed bad pixels.

Robust weighted quadratic blackbody correction and covariance

reference_samples contains frame_id, reference_id, pixel_index, expected_c, and sigma_c. Group by frame and reference_id. Each reference must contain at least two distinct in-range pixels, may not use a bad pixel, and must repeat one finite expected_c and one finite positive sigma_c on every row.

For each reference, x is the median preliminary camera temperature of its sample pixels and y is expected_c. At least five references are required. Base weight is 1/(sigma_c*sigma_c). Let center be the base-weighted mean of x and span the greatest absolute x-center distance. span must be finite and positive. Define z=(x-center)/span and use basis [z*z,z,1].

Fit y=a*z*z+b*z+d with exactly eight Huber IRLS solves. Start every robust weight at 1. For each solve use effective weight = base weight * robust weight, solve the 3 by 3 normal system with Gaussian elimination and partial pivoting, then—except after solve eight—set standardized = abs(prediction-y)/sigma_c and robust weight to 1 when standardized <= 1.5, otherwise 1.5/standardized. Every robust weight must remain finite and positive. Before each elimination, matrixScale is the largest absolute matrix element and every selected pivot must exceed 1e-12*matrixScale.

Convert the final normalized coefficients to original temperature coordinates:

q=a/(span*span)
l=b/span-2*a*center/(span*span)
c=d-b*center/span+a*center*center/(span*span)

The final correction must be strictly increasing at the smallest and largest reference x. weightedRmseC=sqrt(sum(effectiveWeight*residual^2)/sum(effectiveWeight)). reducedChiSquare=sum(effectiveWeight*residual^2)/(referenceCount-3), and must be finite and greater than zero.

Invert the final normalized 3 by 3 normal matrix by solving against each identity column. Multiply that inverse by reducedChiSquare. Transform covariance from [a,b,d] to [q,l,c] with

T=[[1/span^2,0,0],[-2*center/span^2,1/span,0],[center^2/span^2,-center/span,1]]
CovarianceOriginal=T*CovarianceNormalized*transpose(T).

For preliminary temperature x, fitVariance=[x^2,x,1]*CovarianceOriginal*transpose([x^2,x,1]). Values below -1e-9 are invalid; clamp smaller negative roundoff to zero. corrected uncertainty is sqrt(max(0,fitVariance)+detectorNoiseC^2). Apply correctedC=q*x*x+l*x+c. Report q, l, c, weightedRmseC, and reducedChiSquare.


Normative numerical conventions

center and span are computed once from base weights and never recomputed during IRLS. Solve 1 uses robust weights of 1. After solves 1 through 7, update the robust weights from that solve; solve 8 uses the weights produced after solve 7. Do not update weights after solve 8. effectiveWeight, the final normal matrix, weightedRmseC, reducedChiSquare, and covariance all use exactly the weights that entered solve 8.

The coefficient vectors are columns ordered [a,b,d] and [q,l,c]. CovarianceNormalized is exactly reducedChiSquare*inverse(X^T*W8*X); do not use a Huber sandwich covariance, divide by the weight sum, or apply another sigma factor. The transform is CovarianceOriginal=T*CovarianceNormalized*T^T. For v=[x*x,x,1], evaluate fitVariance=v*CovarianceOriginal*v^T. Equivalently:

fitVariance=x^4*Cqq+x^2*Cll+Ccc+2*x^3*Cql+2*x^2*Cqc+2*x*Clc

Here x is the preliminary camera temperature before the blackbody correction. detectorNoiseC is already a one-sigma temperature uncertainty in degrees C; add detectorNoiseC^2 directly and do not multiply it by the correction derivative.

Pixel uncertainties within one frame use the anisotropic projected-space covariance kernel defined below. Do not treat pixels as independent for region-load or Arrhenius-rate variance. Frames from different cameras are independent, but frames from one camera use the temporal covariance kernel defined below. In the Arrhenius calculation, use the corrected furnace temperature once to form kelvin and multiply the stated temperature derivative by uncertaintyC and projected area exactly once before applying the spatial covariance sum. Selection words are literal: greatest means descending numeric order. fastestSignificantThermalLoadRise and steepestSignificantArrheniusAcceleration choose the greatest positive unrounded lower95 value, not the smallest or greatest absolute value.

Furnace coordinates

Apply mirrorX in sensor coordinates, then the clockwise rotation.

rotation 0: output (xm, y), size width by height
rotation 90: output (height - 1 - y, xm), size height by width
rotation 180: output (width - 1 - xm, height - 1 - y), size width by height
rotation 270: output (y, width - 1 - xm), size height by width

The hotspot is the first maximum in row-major furnace order.

Projective physical mapping

The homography H maps a furnace-coordinate point (x,y) to millimetres:

d = h6*x + h7*y + h8
X = (h0*x + h1*y + h2) / d
Y = (h3*x + h4*y + h5) / d

For each output pixel centre (x,y), its cell corners are (x-0.5,y-0.5), (x+0.5,y-0.5), (x+0.5,y+0.5), and (x-0.5,y+0.5). Map every corner through H. Each denominator must be finite and have absolute value greater than 1e-12. The projected cell area is the absolute shoelace area of the four mapped corners. Every area must be finite and greater than zero. projectedAreaMm2 is the sum of all cell areas.

Anisotropic projected-space uncertainty covariance

Use mapped pixel centres, never sensor indices or unprojected furnace coordinates. Let theta=correlationAngleDeg*pi/180. For centres i and j, dx=Xj-Xi and dy=Yj-Yi, then

major=cos(theta)*dx+sin(theta)*dy
minor=-sin(theta)*dx+cos(theta)*dy
rho(i,j)=exp(-0.5*((major/correlationMajorMm)^2+(minor/correlationMinorMm)^2))

rho(i,i)=1 follows from this formula. Every rho must be finite and in (0,1]. For scalar uncertainty contributions s_i over an index set S, evaluate

variance=sum(i in S, s_i^2)+2*sum(i<j in S, s_i*s_j*rho(i,j))
sigma=sqrt(variance)

Include each unordered off-diagonal pair exactly once in the second sum. The variance and sigma must be finite and greater than zero.

Full-field statistics and confidence field

Use unrounded furnace temperatures. meanC is the arithmetic mean. stddevC is the population standard deviation. p95C uses linear interpolation at h=0.95*(N-1). Report meanUncertaintyC and maxUncertaintyC from the oriented uncertainty field. The lower-confidence temperature at each pixel is lowerConfidence[i]=temperatureC[i]-confidenceK*uncertaintyC[i]. The robust threshold is median(lowerConfidence)+hotSigma*1.4826*MAD(lowerConfidence). Build the component mask with the exact rule hot[i]=(lowerConfidence[i] > threshold). Do not use temperatureC[i] > threshold; the unadjusted temperature is used later for integrated excess, peak reporting, and Arrhenius calculations. The hotspot is the first maximum temperature in row-major furnace order and reports its uncertaintyC.

Dominant uncertainty-qualified thermal-load region

Form 8-connected hot components and discard components smaller than minHotArea. For each retained component:

areaMm2=sum(projected cell area)
integratedExcessCmm2=sum((temperatureC-thresholdC)*projected cell area)
loadSigmaCmm2 uses the anisotropic covariance formula with S equal to the component pixels and s_i=uncertaintyC_i*projectedArea_i
lower95IntegratedExcessCmm2=integratedExcessCmm2-1.96*loadSigmaCmm2

Discard components whose lower95IntegratedExcessCmm2 is not finite or is <=0. peak uses greatest lower-confidence temperature, then greatest actual temperature, then smallest row-major index. centroidMm uses weights (lowerConfidence-thresholdC)*projected area and mapped pixel centres. bounds are inclusive output-pixel bounds. Choose the dominant component by greatest unrounded lower95IntegratedExcessCmm2, then integratedExcessCmm2, areaMm2, pixel count, smaller minY, smaller minX, and smaller peak index. hotRegion is null when none remains.

Arrhenius reaction rate and uncertainty

For each output pixel, kelvin=temperatureC+273.15 and ratePerSecond=arrheniusA*exp(-activationEnergyJMol/(8.31446261815324*kelvin)). The temperature derivative is ratePerSecond*activationEnergyJMol/(8.31446261815324*kelvin*kelvin). frame.arrheniusRateMm2PerSecond is sum(ratePerSecond*projected area). frame.arrheniusRateSigmaMm2PerSecond uses the anisotropic covariance formula over every output pixel with s_i=derivative_i*uncertaintyC_i*projectedArea_i. Both must be finite and greater than zero.

Archive summary

Sort frames by capturedAt then frameId. Timestamps must be valid ISO-8601 instants. hottestFrameId uses greatest unrounded maxC, then lexicographically smallest frameId. meanFrameMeanC is the mean of unrounded frame means. largestConservativeRegionFrameId uses greatest unrounded lower95IntegratedExcessCmm2, then integratedExcessCmm2, then frameId. Both conservative-region fields are null if no hot region exists.

For each camera compare consecutive frames with positive elapsed time. load rate=(later integratedExcess-or-zero-earlier integratedExcess-or-zero)/elapsedMinutes. rate sigma=sqrt(earlier loadSigma^2+later loadSigma^2)/elapsedMinutes. lower95 rate=rate-1.96*rate sigma. Keep only positive lower95 rates. fastestSignificantThermalLoadRise uses greatest lower95 rate, then greatest rate, then cameraId, fromFrameId, and toFrameId lexicographically; it is null when none qualify.

For each camera, trapezoidal dose is sum(0.5*(earlierRate+laterRate)*elapsedSeconds). Build one coefficient per frame: each interval adds 0.5*elapsedSeconds to both endpoint coefficients. dose sigma=sqrt(sum((coefficient*frame arrhenius-rate sigma)^2)); dose lower95=dose-1.96*dose sigma. largestConservativeArrheniusDose uses greatest lower95 dose, then greatest dose, then cameraId; it is null when no camera has two frames.


Temporal covariance, GLS acceleration, and dose

For one camera, sort frames by capturedAt then frameId and require strictly increasing instants. Every frame for that camera must report the same finite positive temporalCorrelationSeconds value tau. For frames i and j, let deltaSeconds be the absolute difference between their instants and define

temporalRho(i,j)=exp(-deltaSeconds/tau).

The diagonal is 1. Every temporalRho must be finite and in (0,1]. Frames from different cameras have zero covariance.

For adjacent hot-region load rise, a missing hotRegion contributes load 0 and sigma 0. With elapsedMinutes greater than zero,

rate=(loadLater-loadEarlier)/elapsedMinutes
varianceDifference=sigmaEarlier^2+sigmaLater^2-2*sigmaEarlier*sigmaLater*temporalRho(earlier,later)
sigmaRate=sqrt(max(0,varianceDifference))/elapsedMinutes
lower95Rate=rate-1.96*sigmaRate.

Values below -1e-9 for varianceDifference are invalid; clamp smaller negative roundoff to zero. Keep only positive lower95Rate candidates. Choose fastestSignificantThermalLoadRise by greatest unrounded lower95Rate, then greatest rate, then cameraId, fromFrameId, and toFrameId ascending.

For Arrhenius rates of one camera, build Sigma where

Sigma[i,j]=arrheniusSigma[i]*arrheniusSigma[j]*temporalRho(i,j).

For generalized least squares, use every camera frame when at least two exist. Let t_i be elapsed minutes from the first frame, y_i be arrheniusRateMm2PerSecond, and X_i=[1,t_i]. For numerical stability, build the temporal correlation matrix R, invert R by Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting, and form SigmaInverse[i,j]=RInverse[i,j]/(sigma_i*sigma_j). Do not invert the raw scaled Sigma directly. Then

B=inverse(transpose(X)*SigmaInverse*X)
beta=B*transpose(X)*SigmaInverse*y.

The acceleration is beta[1], its sigma is sqrt(B[1,1]), and lower95 is acceleration-1.96*sigma. The 2 by 2 normal determinant must exceed 1e-12 times the square of its largest absolute element. Keep only positive lower95 candidates. Choose steepestSignificantArrheniusAcceleration by greatest unrounded lower95, then greatest acceleration, then cameraId, first frameId, and last frameId ascending. Report the first and last frame IDs and the number of observations.

For the trapezoidal Arrhenius dose, construct coefficient c_i in seconds by adding half of each adjacent interval to both endpoint frames. dose=sum(c_i*rate_i), varianceDose=transpose(c)*Sigma*c, sigmaDose=sqrt(varianceDose), and lower95Dose=dose-1.96*sigmaDose. Choose largestConservativeArrheniusDose by greatest unrounded lower95Dose, then greatest dose, then cameraId ascending. Temporal correlation, correlation-matrix inversion, GLS covariance, dose variance, and every derived value must be finite; required pivots must exceed 1e-12 times the original correlation-matrix scale.

Output

Write exact top-level keys frames and summary. Each frame has exact keys:

frameId, cameraId, calibrationRevision, geometryRevision, capturedAt, sensorWidth, sensorHeight, width, height, repairedPixels, referenceQuadratic, referenceLinear, referenceOffsetC, referenceWeightedRmseC, referenceReducedChiSquare, projectedAreaMm2, arrheniusRateMm2PerSecond, arrheniusRateSigmaMm2PerSecond, meanUncertaintyC, maxUncertaintyC, minC, maxC, meanC, stddevC, p95C, thresholdC, hotspot, hotRegion

hotspot has x, y, temperatureC, uncertaintyC. A non-null hotRegion has areaPixels, areaMm2, peak, centroidMm, integratedExcessCmm2, loadSigmaCmm2, lower95IntegratedExcessCmm2, and bounds. peak has x, y, temperatureC, uncertaintyC. centroidMm has x and y. bounds has minX, minY, maxX, maxY.

summary has exact keys frameCount, hottestFrameId, globalMaxC, meanFrameMeanC, largestConservativeRegionFrameId, largestLower95IntegratedExcessCmm2, fastestSignificantThermalLoadRise, steepestSignificantArrheniusAcceleration, and largestConservativeArrheniusDose. fastestSignificantThermalLoadRise has cameraId, fromFrameId, toFrameId, rateCmm2PerMinute, sigmaCmm2PerMinute, and lower95Cmm2PerMinute. steepestSignificantArrheniusAcceleration has cameraId, fromFrameId, toFrameId, observationCount, accelerationMm2PerSecondPerMinute, sigmaMm2PerSecondPerMinute, and lower95Mm2PerSecondPerMinute. largestConservativeArrheniusDose has cameraId, doseMm2, sigmaMm2, and lower95Mm2.

Round every reported floating-point number to three decimals using decimal HALF_UP only at JSON publication. Keep integer counts and coordinates as integers. Replace the output atomically only after the entire archive succeeds.
