Exact rules for how a client's clock selection is certified, referenced by the question in instruction.md. Every rule below affects the reward.

Eligibility. Each Candidate records one measurement a client obtained from one server: a correctness interval from lo to hi together with the measured offset. The candidate is eligible only when the server it came from is reachable and that server's stratum is strictly less than 16. A stratum of 16 marks a server that is itself unsynchronized. Ineligible candidates are discarded before anything else happens: they take no part in the selection, they are counted in neither truechimer_count nor falseticker_count, and they never supply a system peer. Let m stand for the number of eligible candidates a client has. Every remaining rule reads only the eligible candidates.

Coverage. A position p on the offset timeline is covered by an eligible candidate exactly when lo is less than or equal to p and p is less than or equal to hi. The coverage of a position is the number of eligible candidates that cover it. Coverage changes only at the lo and hi values of the eligible candidates, so those endpoint positions are the only ones that need to be examined.

The tolerated intersection. The certification tolerates as few disagreeing servers as possible. Consider the whole numbers f taken in increasing order 0, 1, 2, and so on, subject to two times f being strictly less than m. For a given f let the threshold be m minus f. The intersection for that f is the range from the smallest endpoint position whose coverage is at least the threshold to the largest endpoint position whose coverage is at least the threshold; if no position reaches the threshold there is no intersection for that f. Count the eligible candidates whose measured offset lies outside that range. The value of f is accepted when an intersection exists and the number of those outside offsets is at most f. The smallest accepted f is the one that governs the client, and its range is the agreed intersection. If no f with two times f strictly less than m is accepted, the client has no agreed intersection.

Truechimers and falsetickers. Using the agreed intersection, an eligible candidate is a truechimer when its own measured offset lies within the intersection, that is when the low end of the intersection is less than or equal to the offset and the offset is less than or equal to the high end. It is a falseticker otherwise. Whether the candidate's interval overlaps the intersection does not matter; only the position of its offset does. So truechimer_count is the number of eligible candidates whose offset lies in the intersection and falseticker_count is the number whose offset lies outside it, and the two counts always add up to m.

The system peer and the derived stratum. Among the truechimers of a client, the system peer is the candidate whose server has the lowest stratum. If several truechimers tie on stratum, the peer is the one whose server has the smallest root_dispersion. If several still tie on both, the peer is the one whose server has the lexicographically smallest name. These three keys are applied in that order and they decide the peer outright; no other property, and in particular no identifier and no insertion order, takes any part in the choice. The reported stratum of the client is its system peer's server stratum plus one.

Unsynchronized clients. A client with no truechimer at all is unsynchronized. This covers a client with no eligible candidate, a client with no agreed intersection, and a client whose intersection contains none of its offsets. An unsynchronized client is still reported: its stratum is 16 and its system_peer is the literal text NONE. Its truechimer_count is 0 and its falseticker_count is its own m.

Numbers. All of lo, hi, offset, stratum and root_dispersion are exact integers, lo is never greater than hi, and offset always lies within lo and hi. Offsets may be negative and an interval may be a single position when lo and hi are equal. No value in this task is a float and no tolerance applies anywhere.
